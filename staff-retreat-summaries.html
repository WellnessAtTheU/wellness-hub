01 · Before the retreat
The pre-survey baseline

Before May 8, staff told us they believed in the work but couldn't always see the map. Connection to the mission sat at a moderate 3.48 — and the real gap was strategic roadmap awareness, at just 2.82 out of 5. The pre-survey named it plainly.

"A translation problem, not a strategy problem."

People were bought in. What they needed was the direction made legible — the strategy translated into something they could see, say, and act on. That became the retreat's job.

What they told us — in their own words
No group · direction & load
The barrier was direction, not the day — several noted the mission and roadmap "were not clear" and should have been stated plainly up front. Underneath ran a workload signal: a request for "more support around burnout prevention and understanding the level of acuity being treated," and time pressure sharp enough that one respondent had to cancel a student appointment when the day ran long. For stretched clinical staff, collaboration reads as one more demand unless its purpose is clear.
Maybe group · wanted more connection
This group wants to connect — the format just didn't get them there. They asked repeatedly for structured, early cross-department interaction: mixed or assigned seating "from the beginning," get-to-know-you time, breakouts instead of lecture, and a take-home list of contacts and next steps. Several named the table-switching activity as the best part of the day. A winnable group — and they told us how.

Roadmap awareness — the biggest gap going in — was the largest single change in the dataset. The translation gap the pre-survey identified is the gap the retreat closed.

The headline
The retreat translated strategy into shared direction. Mission connection rose, roadmap awareness rose most, and 67% of staff left ready to work across teams — moving a divisions-under-one-roof idea a real step toward a division that acts like one.
Where the work goes next
The retreat is a moment; what we build after is the work. Follow-up concentrates on the clinical and wellness teams — UCC, SHC, CDA, and CCW — pairing two moves: communicating the mission and roadmap clearly and repeatedly, and standing up concrete connection structures (named contacts, cross-team groups). And it treats clinical workload as a genuine barrier, not a reluctance to collaborate.
